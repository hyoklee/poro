# Concept: induced seismicity and the Pohang EGS case

## Definition

Injecting fluid underground raises pore pressure on nearby faults; if a fault is already close to failure, the pressure increase can trigger slip. The size of the resulting earthquake is set by the fault and its stored tectonic stress, not by the injected volume, so small injections can unlock large events. Pressure keeps diffusing after injection stops, so the risk window outlasts the pumping.

## Pohang, South Korea

South Korea's first enhanced geothermal system (EGS), near Pohang, drilled two wells (PX-1, PX-2) to about 4.2 km into granite and ran five hydraulic stimulation rounds from January 2016 to September 2017, injecting roughly 12,800 cubic metres in total for a planned 1.2 MW plant. On 2017-11-15, about two months after the last injection, a magnitude 5.4 earthquake struck with its hypocentre next to PX-2: about 135 injured, over 1,700 displaced, tens of thousands of buildings damaged, roughly 300 million USD in losses, and the national college entrance exam postponed. A government-commissioned international panel reported in March 2019 that injection had raised pore pressure on an unmapped, critically stressed fault; a M3.1 event in April 2017 during stimulation had not stopped operations. The project was cancelled and the wells sealed in 2019; a 2023 court ordered compensation to residents.

## Why it belongs in this wiki

PoroTomo measured how a pressure front actually travels through a fractured reservoir ([concept-poroelastic-tomography.md](concept-poroelastic-tomography.md)). Pohang is the consequence of not knowing that before injecting: the protocols that followed stress fault mapping before injection, pressure-diffusion modelling, and treating post-injection migration as a hazard window.
